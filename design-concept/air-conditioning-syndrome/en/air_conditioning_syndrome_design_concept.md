# Design Concept: Air-Conditioning Syndrome (냉방병)

## 1. Overview

- **Topic**: Air-conditioning syndrome / cooling sickness (냉방병) — a common, non-severe but widespread summer health condition caused by prolonged exposure to strong air conditioning (cold-like symptoms, digestive upset, fatigue, headache).
- **Deliverable**: A health-education 카드뉴스 (card news) carousel for social media (Instagram-style square/portrait format), following the same deliverable convention as this project's prior [Summer 3-frame card news concept](../../summer-card-news/en/summer_card_news_design_concept.md).
- **Assumed audience/tone**: No specific brand or audience was given. Because the topic is a symptom/cause/prevention health topic rather than a mood piece, this concept assumes a general SNS health-information audience (the same audience the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency, KDCA, targets with its own card news on this exact topic — see §2). The tone should be approachable and reassuring, not alarming — 냉방병 is common and easily prevented, not a medical emergency.
- **Format assumption**: A 4–5 frame Instagram carousel (1080×1080 primary, adaptable to 1080×1350), longer than the prior 3-frame summer piece because the content itself is information-dense (symptoms → causes → prevention → summary), matching the structure KDCA's own reference card news uses (see §2).

## 2. Trend & Reference Research

**Direct topical reference — KDCA's own 냉방병 card news**: The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (질병관리청) publishes a card news titled "에어컨 때문에 생기는 건강 문제, 냉방병을 조심하세요!" (["Be careful of air-conditioning syndrome"](https://www.kdca.go.kr/bbs/kdca/45/311654/artclView.do?layout=unknown)). It runs 5 frames — intro/hook, symptom identification, causes, five prevention tips, and a closing summary — using a friendly mascot character, a bright and accessible color palette, large text, and simple illustrated icons rather than photography. This is the single most relevant reference available: it's the same government body's own communication on this exact condition, and it validates both the frame count and the "friendly, not clinical-cold" tone as the right fit for this topic.

**KDCA's card news portal (국가건강정보포털)**: The [broader card news library](https://health.kdca.go.kr/healthinfo/biz/health/ntcnInfo/healthSourc/cardNews/cardNewsMain.do) this piece belongs to uses a consistent grid of thumbnail+title cards, filterable by age group, confirming that KDCA's health card news treats each condition as a self-contained short narrative rather than a single infographic — reinforcing the multi-frame carousel format over a single static graphic.

**2026 healthcare/wellness color trend — "Synthesized Mint"**: [AND Academy's 2026 color trend report](https://www.andacademy.com/resources/blog/graphic-design/color-trends-for-designers/) names "Synthesized Mint" (Dusty Mint, Pale Pistachio, Clinical Green) as a defining 2026 wellness-industry color direction — described as "medical-grade greens" that read as clean and restorative rather than sterile-white-and-blue. This is a more current, less generic choice than default clinical blue for a health topic.

**2026 healthcare web design trends**: [Orbix Studio's 2026 healthcare web design trends](https://www.orbix.studio/blogs/healthcare-web-design-trends) and related industry reporting converge on: accessibility-first (WCAG-conscious contrast and type size), mobile-first delivery, and — specifically for wellness-oriented content — soothing, nature-derived color schemes over sterile clinical-white/blue, aimed at lowering a reader's anxiety rather than looking authoritative for its own sake. This supports choosing a warmer, softer palette over stark medical blue.

**2026 healthcare infographic design pattern**: [DesignRush's 2026 healthcare infographic showcase](https://www.designrush.com/best-designs/infographics/trends/healthcare-infographics) highlights a recurring, effective pattern across award-winning medical infographics: a restrained neutral/gray base with a single bold accent color used specifically to flag the important point (e.g. "The Piccolo Occluder" by John Devolle uses high-contrast orange on minimal line art specifically to make a complex medical procedure "surprisingly approachable"). This "one accent, one job" principle — rather than a busy multi-color scheme — is directly applicable to flagging 냉방병's warning symptoms against a calm base.

**A restrained, trust-signaling counterexample**: [Vibrant Wellness](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/vibrant-wellness), an Awwwards Site of the Day (Feb 2026), builds its entire visual identity from just two blues (#8EBEF9, #0774C4). It's cited here not for its color choice directly (blue reads more "clinical" than this topic calls for) but for the underlying principle it demonstrates well: a minimal, disciplined 2–3 color palette communicates medical trust more effectively than a decorative multi-color one — a principle this concept borrows while swapping the hue family to mint/coral (see §3–4).

**Format/typography precedent within this project**: This project's own prior [Summer 3-frame card news concept](../../summer-card-news/en/summer_card_news_design_concept.md) established Pretendard as the house typeface for card news (single variable Korean font, Black/ExtraBold for hooks, Medium/Regular for body) and a "consistent margin/logo placement across frames" convention for carousel continuity. This concept reuses both for consistency across the project's card news output, while diverging in color direction and illustration mood — reasoned in §3.

**Topic facts** (used to keep content medically accurate, not a design source): [서울대학교병원 의학정보 — 냉방병](https://www.snuh.org/health/nMedInfo/nView.do?category=DIS&medid=AA000488) confirms 냉방병's actual mechanism (indoor/outdoor temperature gaps of 5–8°C+ straining the autonomic nervous system) and symptoms (nasal/throat irritation, digestive upset, fatigue) — relevant to what the card news content itself should say, informing which points the design needs to give visual emphasis to.

## 3. Design Concept Statement

**"Cool, Not Too Cool" (적정 냉방 · 시원함의 균형)**

The concept is built from the specific irony at the center of this topic: air conditioning is not the enemy — it's a genuine comfort — but *too much* of that comfort is exactly what causes the illness. This distinguishes the concept from a generic "healthy blue/clean" health-content palette (which would suit almost any health topic and therefore says nothing specific about this one): here, the "good" color (a soft, restorative mint, per the 2026 wellness color trend in §2) represents the AC's genuine relief, while a warm coral/amber accent is reserved *specifically* for the moment the content flags a warning sign or a "too far" threshold — mirroring the KDCA reference's structure of "identify the symptom" as a distinct narrative beat. The palette itself enacts the card news' core message: cool is good, until one specific accent color says otherwise.

Following the "one accent, one job" principle from the DesignRush reference (§2) and the minimal-palette discipline the Vibrant Wellness reference demonstrates, the coral accent is used sparingly and consistently only for symptom/warning callouts and the closing prevention CTA — never as a decorative color — so it retains its signal value across all frames.

Illustration follows KDCA's own reference more than the prior summer piece's textured/hand-drawn illustration mood: flat, simple line icons rather than grain-textured illustration, because this is instructional health content (clarity first) rather than a mood-driven seasonal promo — a deliberate divergence from the summer card news' "authenticity over polish" texture, justified by the accessibility-first healthcare trend in §2.

## 4. Color Palette

| Role | Color | Hex | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Background (neutral) | Warm Ivory | `#FBF8F3` | Keeps the palette approachable rather than sterile-white; consistent with this project's card news background convention, and prevents the mint/coral accents from reading cold. |
| Primary / "good cool" | Dusty Mint | `#8FC7B8` | Directly from the 2026 "Synthesized Mint" wellness trend (§2) — represents AC's genuine comfort/relief; used as the dominant field color on frames describing normal, healthy cooling. |
| Secondary / structure & trust | Deep Teal | `#2E6E68` | A deeper shade of the same mint family (not a second unrelated hue) — used for headline text and section dividers, giving the palette the disciplined 2–3 color restraint the Vibrant Wellness reference demonstrates, while staying in the mint family established as the wellness signal. |
| Warning / "too far" accent | Warm Coral | `#FF7A59` | Reserved exclusively for symptom callouts, warning icons, and the prevention-tips CTA — the "one accent, one job" color per the DesignRush reference (§2). Never used decoratively, so it keeps its meaning as "pay attention here." |
| Neutral structure | Cool Stone Gray | `#C7CFCC` | For dividers, secondary text panels, and icon backgrounds — a quiet neutral that doesn't compete with the mint/coral signal colors. |
| Text ink | Charcoal Navy | `#26332E` | A warm-dark neutral (not pure black) for body copy, legible against Warm Ivory without feeling clinical. |

## 5. Typography

**Pretendard** (Black/ExtraBold for frame headlines and hook text, Medium for body copy, SemiBold for symptom/warning callout labels) — reused from this project's prior card news concept (§2) for cross-deliverable consistency, and independently well-suited here: it's a free, commercially licensed variable Korean font with a wide weight range, so the same single file can carry both the large hook headline and the smaller, denser symptom/prevention list text this topic requires without needing a second typeface.

## 6. Layout & UI Patterns

Designed for 1080×1080 primary, safe-area-adjusted for 1080×1350, in a 5-frame structure directly modeled on the KDCA reference (§2):

- **Frame 1 (Hook)**: Dusty Mint dominant field, a large Pretendard Black headline posing the topic conversationally (e.g. "여름 감기인 줄 알았는데?" — "Thought it was just a summer cold?"), consistent with KDCA's conversational-question hook style. Minimal supporting copy.
- **Frame 2 (Symptoms)**: Warm Ivory background, symptoms laid out as a simple icon + short-label list (not paragraphs); each symptom icon paired with a small Warm Coral dot/tag to flag it as a "watch for this" item — this is where the accent color's "one job" begins.
- **Frame 3 (Causes)**: Dusty Mint or Cool Stone Gray background, a simple before/after or indoor-vs-outdoor temperature-gap visual (a direct, simple diagram rather than illustration-heavy), Deep Teal used for the explanatory headline.
- **Frame 4 (Prevention tips)**: Warm Ivory background, tips in a spacious single-column checklist (icon + short line per tip, not dense paragraphs) — accessibility-first per §2, avoiding a cramped multi-column layout.
- **Frame 5 (Closing/CTA)**: Warm Coral accent block (banner or button-style) against Deep Teal or Warm Ivory, restating the core message ("적정 냉방으로 건강한 여름 보내기") plus a save/share prompt.
- **Cross-frame consistency**: identical logo/handle placement and outer margin on all five frames, reused from the prior card news concept's carousel-continuity convention (§2).
- **Responsive note**: keep text and the logo mark inside the safe area shared by both 1:1 and 4:5 crops.

## 7. Imagery & Iconography

- **Style**: Flat, simple line icons (AC unit, thermometer, sneeze, stomach/digestion, blanket/long sleeve) — no photography, no grain texture, no 3D — a deliberate divergence from the prior summer card news' hand-drawn texture, justified by the accessibility-first, clarity-over-mood nature of instructional health content (§2–3).
- **Color treatment**: Icons rendered in Deep Teal (default state) or Warm Coral (warning/symptom state) as duotone-style single-color fills, never full multi-color illustration — keeps the "one accent, one job" discipline visible at the icon level, not just the background level.
- **Mascot note**: KDCA's own reference uses a friendly mascot character; this concept does not require one, but if a lighter, non-clinical mascot or character element is wanted, it should stay within the mint/coral palette rather than introducing a new hue family.

## 8. Motion/Interaction Notes

Static carousel; motion is intentionally minimal. As with the prior summer card news concept, consistent logo/margin placement across all five frames is what carries the "continuity" feel through the swipe gesture rather than any animated transition — kept deliberately simple since the content's job is clarity, not spectacle.
