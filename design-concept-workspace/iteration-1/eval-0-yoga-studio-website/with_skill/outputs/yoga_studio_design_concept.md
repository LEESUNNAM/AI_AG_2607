# Design Concept: Yoga Studio Brand Website

## 1. Overview

- **Topic**: A yoga studio brand website (marketing/landing-style site — class info, philosophy, booking, instructor introductions).
- **Target audience**: Women in their 20s and 30s, as specified by the client.
- **Desired tone**: Healing, comfortable, and restorative ("힐링/편안한 느낌") — the client explicitly wants the site to feel like a retreat from daily stress, not a high-performance fitness brand.
- **Assumptions made**: Since no specific studio name, class style (hot yoga, vinyasa, restorative, etc.), or existing brand assets were given, this concept treats the studio as a general boutique/urban yoga studio rather than a hardcore fitness-athletic one, since "healing/comfortable" points away from the aggressive, high-energy positioning seen in gym-style yoga chains (e.g. CorePower Yoga's bright orange/black "electric energy" branding). If the studio is actually a hot-yoga or power-yoga specialist, the palette and copy tone below should be dialed slightly warmer/more energetic.

## 2. Trend & Reference Research

### General 2026 web design trends relevant to this brief
- Wellness-specific sites in 2026 are moving toward being "digital sanctuaries" — calm, high-performance sites that must still convert to bookings, not just look pretty (SMPLY Studio, 2026 Web Design Trends for Wellness Brands).
- Clean lines, muted tones, and strong real (non-stock) studio photography are repeatedly cited as what separates good yoga sites from generic ones (Colorlib, 27 Best Yoga Studio Website Examples 2026).
- Korean-market trend coverage for 2026 highlights "고기능성 미니멀리즘" (high-functioning minimalism) as a leading wellness/beauty keyword — consumers want honest, ingredient/process-transparent, minimal-but-substantial brand experiences, not maximalist decoration (Harper's Bazaar Korea, "2026 여기를 주목하라! 웰니스&뷰티 키워드 8").
- Broader 2026 web design trend coverage (grain textures, glassmorphism, layered depth, micro-interactions) is more aligned with bold/editorial or tech-brand sites; for a healing-focused yoga brand, only the "subtle grain/texture over flat color" element and light micro-interactions are relevant — bold asymmetry and saturated color are not, since they cut against the calm/restorative brief (Designkits, "2026년 웹디자인 트렌드 총정리").

### Reference sites (real, verified via Awwwards and design roundups)

1. **YOGAMAYA (NYC)** — Awwwards Honorable Mention — https://www.awwwards.com/sites/yogamaya
   Uses a warm cream (~#EBDCC2) against a deep teal-green (~#0B2A2B), full-screen single-page layout with unusual navigation and an extravagant serif display type (Dahlia). Relevant because it proves a "sophisticated, editorial" earth-tone approach can still feel warm rather than corporate — though its teal/near-black contrast reads slightly more mature/upscale than the "comfortable" tone this brief wants, so it's used here as a reference for pairing warm neutrals with one deep grounding color, not for its exact hues.

2. **Yoga by Nina** — Awwwards Honorable Mention (July 2022) — https://www.awwwards.com/sites/yoga-by-nina
   Warm tan (~#CCBB99) paired with deep charcoal (~#111111), minimal typography, video hero and about sections. Relevant for its "uncluttered, meditative" UX pattern — generous negative space and a restrained two-tone palette carrying the entire emotional register, which is exactly the discipline a healing-brand site needs so it doesn't compete visually with the calming message.

3. **Highland Yoga** (cited in Wild Berry Studio's roundup) — https://www.wildberry.studio/blog/best-yoga-websites-design-inspiration
   Built around shades of teal as its signature accent. Relevant as a counter-example: teal alone can feel slightly clinical/cool for a "warm, healing" brief, which is why this concept borrows only a muted sage-green rather than teal.

4. **Living Barre & Yoga** (same roundup) — https://www.wildberry.studio/blog/best-yoga-websites-design-inspiration
   Uses light seashell pink as a primary tone. Directly relevant to the 20-30대 여성 target: a soft, warm pink-beige reads approachable and gentle without being juvenile, and is a closer match to "comfortable" than stark black/white minimalism.

5. **HERSPACE** (same roundup) — https://www.wildberry.studio/blog/best-yoga-websites-design-inspiration
   Warm earthy reddish-brown palette with bold, characterful type. Relevant as the source for this concept's terracotta accent — a way to add warmth and a touch of energy without breaking the muted, grounded palette.

6. **MASAJ** (cited in Haute Stock's wellness palette roundup) — https://hautestock.co/luxury-color-palettes-for-wellness-brands/
   A massage-studio site using muted grey/white with terracotta accents for an "understated luxury" feel. Confirms terracotta-as-accent (rather than primary) is a proven pattern for calm-but-not-boring wellness sites, which is exactly the accent role terracotta plays in the palette below.

Coverage of Korean-market yoga/Pilates studio sites specifically was thin — most discoverable Korean-language results were activewear commerce brands (필로소피아, 아사나) rather than boutique studio marketing sites with notable design, so this concept leans on the international yoga-studio and wellness-branding references above rather than fabricating local examples.

## 3. Design Concept Statement

**Keyword: "Soft Sanctuary" (포근한 안식처)** — secondary phrase: **Quiet Warmth (조용한 온기)**

The brief's own language — "healing," "comfortable," 20-30대 여성 — points away from two common yoga-site defaults: the black-and-neon "athletic performance" look (CorePower Yoga's orange/black energy) and the stark white/grey "clinical wellness" look (plain minimalist spas). Neither reads as *comforting*. Instead, the research above (Living Barre's seashell pink, HERSPACE's earthy terracotta, Yoga by Nina's restrained tan-and-charcoal, and the 2026 "고기능성 미니멀리즘" trend of honest, unembellished brand experiences) converges on a warm, muted, skin-tone-adjacent neutral palette carried by generous whitespace: minimal in structure, warm and tactile in color and texture. "Soft Sanctuary" means the site itself should function like the studio's reception area — quiet, uncluttered, warm underfoot — so that by the time a visitor reaches the booking button they already feel the calm the studio promises, rather than having to be convinced of it through decoration.

## 4. Color Palette

| Role | Color | Hex | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary / Background | Warm Ivory | `#F6EFE7` | A soft, warm off-white (not stark white) that reads calm and skin-warm rather than clinical — the base "room" of the site, echoing the warm cream base seen in YOGAMAYA and the pink-beige base in Living Barre. |
| Secondary | Dusty Blush | `#E8C9C0` | A muted pink-beige (per Living Barre) used for section backgrounds, cards, and soft dividers — reads gentle and approachable to the 20-30대 여성 target without tipping into "girly"/juvenile territory. |
| Accent | Terracotta | `#C77B58` | Drawn from HERSPACE and MASAJ's terracotta accents — used sparingly on CTAs, links, and small highlights to add warmth and a touch of energy/appetite-to-act against an otherwise quiet palette. |
| Grounding / Secondary Accent | Muted Sage | `#8A9A80` | A desaturated, warm-leaning green (deliberately softer than Highland Yoga's teal, which read too cool/clinical for this brief) — used for icons, secondary buttons, and nature/breath-related motifs. |
| Text / Deep Neutral | Warm Charcoal | `#3A332E` | A warm near-black (not pure `#000`) for body copy and headlines, softer on the eye than true black — inspired by Yoga by Nina's `#111111` but warmed to match the ivory/blush base rather than contrasting starkly against it. |
| Structural Neutral | Sand Grey | `#B8AFA6` | For borders, disabled states, and fine UI lines — keeps the interface quiet where teal/black would otherwise be the only "hard" edges available. |

## 5. Typography

- **Headings**: **Fraunces** (Google Fonts) — a soft-contrast serif with warm, slightly rounded terminals. It gives the same "editorial warmth" that YOGAMAYA's Dahlia display serif provides, signaling craft and calm sophistication rather than gym-brand boldness, while staying free and web-safe (unlike Dahlia, which is a paid/custom face).
- **Body / UI**: **Pretendard** — the de facto standard Korean web font, chosen for its clean, neutral, highly legible letterforms across Korean and Latin text, matching the "clean, minimal typefaces" pattern seen across Open Studios/Ourano Yoga and Yoga by Nina's restrained typographic approach. Pretendard's rounded-adjacent weights also keep body text feeling soft rather than mechanical.
- **Pairing rationale**: A warm serif for emotional, brand-carrying moments (hero headline, section titles, instructor quotes) paired with a quiet, highly legible sans for everything functional (class schedules, booking forms, navigation) — so the "healing" feeling lives in the display type while the UI stays fast and easy to scan on mobile, where most bookings will happen.

## 6. Layout & UI Patterns

- **Grid density**: Spacious, not dense. Generous vertical whitespace between sections (per the "uncluttered, meditative" pattern seen in Yoga by Nina and InnSaei Studio/Alma in the Wild Berry roundup) — the site should never feel like a class-schedule spreadsheet, even on the timetable page.
- **Navigation**: A simple top nav (Home / Classes / Instructors / Pricing / Booking / Contact) with a soft, low-contrast sticky header (ivory background, no hard drop shadow) rather than a dense mega-menu — keeps the "quiet" feeling even while navigating.
- **Hero section**: Full-width, either a slow-panning studio/practice video or a large still photograph (real studio photography, not stock, per Colorlib's finding that this is what separates strong yoga sites from generic ones), with the headline set in Fraunces over a soft gradient scrim in Warm Ivory/Warm Charcoal for legibility.
- **Cards & content blocks**: Soft-cornered cards (12–16px radius) on Dusty Blush or Warm Ivory backgrounds for class types and instructor bios — echoing the "elliptical/soft-frame" imagery pattern noted in the Wild Berry roundup as a way to break up rigid rectangles without adding visual noise.
- **Booking flow**: Should be reachable within one scroll/tap from anywhere (sticky "Book a Class" CTA in Terracotta) — 2026 wellness-site guidance stresses that the calming aesthetic must still convert, not just look good (SMPLY Studio; ourearth.co.kr's guidance on real-time booking integration for Korean fitness/yoga sites).
- **Responsive behavior**: Mobile-first single column with the hero video/image cropped to a tall aspect rather than cut off; class schedule collapses into a swipeable day-by-day card list rather than a dense table, since a data-table timetable would break the calm mood on small screens.

## 7. Imagery & Iconography

- **Photography over illustration**: Real, warmly-lit studio and practice photography (natural window light, muted wood/linen textures in the shots themselves) rather than stock photography or 3D-rendered graphics — directly supported by Colorlib's finding that "striking studio photography" (not generic stock) is a recurring trait of the strongest yoga sites.
- **Color treatment**: Light, warm-toned grading (slightly lifted shadows, warm white balance) so photography sits naturally within the Warm Ivory/Dusty Blush palette rather than looking color-corrected against it.
- **Texture**: A very subtle grain/paper texture overlay on large color blocks (a restrained nod to the 2026 "layered depth/grain" trend from Designkits) — enough to feel tactile and handmade, not enough to read as a "trendy" gimmick that would date quickly.
- **Iconography**: Simple, thin-line icons (1.5px stroke) for class types, amenities, and schedule/booking UI — line icons read lighter and calmer than filled/bold icon sets, consistent with the muted, quiet visual language throughout.

## 8. Motion/Interaction Notes

Keep motion minimal and slow rather than snappy: gentle fade/slide-up reveals on scroll (300–500ms, eased), a slow ambient loop or subtle parallax on the hero image/video, and soft hover states (color shift, not scale/bounce) on buttons and cards. The interaction feel should mirror breath — slow, unhurried transitions — rather than the fast, punchy micro-interactions common on fitness/gym sites; this is a deliberate, brief-driven exception to the more "energetic" animation trends noted in general 2026 coverage.
