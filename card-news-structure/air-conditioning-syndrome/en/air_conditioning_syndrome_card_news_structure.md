# Content Structure: Air-Conditioning Syndrome (냉방병) — 10-Section Card News

## 1. Overview

- **Deliverable**: A 10-section 카드뉴스 (card news) carousel, extending this project's prior [design concept](../../../design-concept/air-conditioning-syndrome/en/air_conditioning_syndrome_design_concept.md) — Section 1 doubles as the post's thumbnail/cover, Sections 2–10 carry the body content in a **기승전결** (four-act: setup → development → turn → conclusion) narrative arc.
- **Section 1**: Thumbnail/cover — the single image representing the whole post in-feed, so its job is pure curiosity, not information delivery.
- **Sections 2–10 (9 sections) mapped to 기승전결**:
  - **기 (Setup)** — §2–3: relatable symptoms, then naming the condition
  - **승 (Development)** — §4–6: symptom detail, then a 감기 vs. 냉방병 distinction
  - **전 (Turn)** — §7–8: the causal mechanism (the "aha"), then personal risk relevance
  - **결 (Conclusion)** — §9–10: prevention checklist, then summary/CTA
- This structure carries forward the design concept's visual grammar: **Dusty Mint** for "normal/comfort" content, **Warm Coral** reserved for warning/symptom flags, **Deep Teal** for explanatory headlines — see the design concept doc for exact hex values.

## 2. Section-by-Section Structure

### Section 1 — Thumbnail (표지)
- **Act**: N/A (cover)
- **Purpose**: Stop the scroll and create curiosity without giving away the answer.
- **Headline draft**: "여름 감기인 줄 알았는데... 사실은?" ("Thought it was just a summer cold... but was it?")
- **Content**: Headline only, no body copy. A single symptom cue (e.g. a sneeze or chill icon) hints at the topic without naming 냉방병 yet — the reveal is Section 3's job.
- **Visual cue**: Dusty Mint dominant field; the AC unit or thermometer icon can appear small in a corner as a visual tease.

### Section 2 — Relatable Symptoms (공감 유도)
- **Act**: 기 (Setup)
- **Purpose**: Get the reader nodding along before the condition is even named — the classic card news "problem before solution" opener.
- **Headline draft**: "요즘 유독 피곤하고, 콧물 나고, 소화도 안 된다면?" ("Unusually tired, sniffly, and your stomach's off lately?")
- **Content**: 3 everyday complaints listed casually (not yet framed as symptoms of a named condition) — fatigue, sniffles, poor digestion.
- **Visual cue**: Warm Ivory background, Deep Teal text; no Coral yet — this section stays in "relatable," not "alarming."

### Section 3 — Naming the Condition (문제 정의)
- **Act**: 기 (Setup)
- **Purpose**: The reveal — connect Section 2's symptoms to a name, defining what 냉방병 actually is.
- **Headline draft**: "그거, 감기가 아니라 '냉방병'일 수 있어요" ("That might not be a cold — it could be air-conditioning syndrome")
- **Content**: One-line definition: prolonged exposure to strong air conditioning causes cold-like symptoms. Not a formal diagnosis name, but a widely recognized condition.
- **Visual cue**: Dusty Mint background, headline in Deep Teal — introduces the AC-unit icon as the recurring visual motif for the rest of the set.

### Section 4 — Symptoms Part 1: Respiratory & Muscular (주요 증상 ①)
- **Act**: 승 (Development)
- **Purpose**: First half of the symptom detail, building out what Section 2 only hinted at.
- **Headline draft**: "이런 증상, 겪고 계신가요?" ("Experiencing these?")
- **Content**: Icon + short-label list — nasal congestion/runny nose, sore throat, headache, chills, muscle aches.
- **Visual cue**: Warm Ivory background; each symptom icon in Deep Teal paired with a small Warm Coral tag — the "watch for this" signal introduced in the design concept begins here.

### Section 5 — Symptoms Part 2: Digestive & Energy (주요 증상 ②)
- **Act**: 승 (Development)
- **Purpose**: Second half of the symptom list — rounds out the picture so the reader has full self-check coverage.
- **Headline draft**: "몸이 무겁고, 속도 더부룩하다면" ("Feeling sluggish and bloated?")
- **Content**: Icon + short-label list — indigestion, abdominal discomfort, loss of appetite, fatigue/lethargy.
- **Visual cue**: Same treatment as Section 4 for visual consistency (Warm Ivory + Coral symptom tags) — Sections 4–5 should read as one continuous two-part list, not two unrelated frames.

### Section 6 — Cold vs. Air-Conditioning Syndrome (감기와 어떻게 다를까?)
- **Act**: 승 (Development)
- **Purpose**: A distinguishing insight that adds value beyond a plain symptom list — the point most readers don't already know, which is what makes a card news worth sharing.
- **Headline draft**: "감기랑 뭐가 다르지?" ("So how is this different from a cold?")
- **Content**: The key differentiator — 냉방병 symptoms typically ease once AC exposure stops, and fever is uncommon, unlike a true cold/flu.
- **Visual cue**: A simple two-column or side-by-side comparison layout (감기 vs. 냉방병), Deep Teal for structure, Warm Coral only on the single most important differentiating word.

### Section 7 — Why It Happens (원인)
- **Act**: 전 (Turn)
- **Purpose**: The "aha" moment — explains the mechanism, reframing the whole topic: it's not the cold air itself, it's the *gap*.
- **Headline draft**: "시원한 게 문제가 아니라, '너무' 시원한 게 문제예요" ("It's not the cool air — it's the gap that's too big")
- **Content**: When the indoor/outdoor temperature difference exceeds roughly 5–8°C, the autonomic nervous system struggles to adjust, straining circulation and lowering immunity — this is the actual cause of the symptoms in §4–5.
- **Visual cue**: A simple indoor-vs-outdoor temperature diagram (a direct icon-based visual, not illustration-heavy), Dusty Mint (outdoor/normal) against a cooler-toned block (indoor), Deep Teal headline.

### Section 8 — Am I at Risk? (나도 위험군일까?)
- **Act**: 전 (Turn)
- **Purpose**: Converts the general explanation into personal relevance — the turn from "here's a fact" to "this might be about you," which motivates reading through to the prevention section.
- **Headline draft**: "혹시 나도 해당될까?" ("Could this be me?")
- **Content**: Risk-factor checklist — office workers who sit under AC vents for long hours, people who move frequently between hot outdoor and cold indoor spaces, and those with lower baseline immunity (fatigue, poor sleep).
- **Visual cue**: Checkbox-style icons, Warm Coral checkmarks to keep the "pay attention" signal consistent with §4–5's symptom tags.

### Section 9 — Prevention Checklist (예방 수칙)
- **Act**: 결 (Conclusion)
- **Purpose**: The payoff — concrete, actionable steps, delivered as the spacious single-column checklist the design concept specifies.
- **Headline draft**: "이렇게 예방하세요" ("Here's how to prevent it")
- **Content**: 5 tips — (1) keep indoor/outdoor temperature gap within 5–6°C, (2) ventilate every 1–2 hours, (3) keep a cardigan/long sleeve on hand for AC-heavy spaces, (4) drink warm tea/fluids regularly, (5) avoid overexertion and keep sleep consistent to support immunity.
- **Visual cue**: Warm Ivory background, one icon + one line per tip, Deep Teal icons (this section is "solution," not "warning," so Coral is intentionally minimized here per the design concept's "one accent, one job" rule).

### Section 10 — Summary & CTA (마무리)
- **Act**: 결 (Conclusion)
- **Purpose**: Closes the loop back to Section 1's hook and gives the reader a next action.
- **Headline draft**: "적정 냉방으로, 건강한 여름 보내세요" ("Keep it just cool enough — for a healthy summer")
- **Content**: One-line recap of the core message plus a save/share prompt (e.g. "저장해두고 실천해보세요" / "Save this and put it into practice").
- **Visual cue**: Warm Coral accent block (banner/button) against Deep Teal or Warm Ivory, per the design concept's Frame 5 spec — logo/handle placement identical to all other sections for carousel continuity.

## 3. Cross-Section Consistency Notes

- **경고 신호는 Warm Coral로만 표시**: only Sections 4, 5, 8, and 10 use the Coral accent (symptom flags, risk checkmarks, and the CTA) — Sections 1–3, 6–7, and 9 stay in the mint/teal "calm" register, keeping the accent's signal value intact across all 10 sections, per the design concept's "one accent, one job" principle.
- **Copy length**: keep each section's headline under ~12 words and body copy under ~25 words total, consistent with general card news copywriting guidance — Section 1 (thumbnail) should be the shortest of all.
- **Narrative callback**: Section 10's headline directly answers the tension set up in Section 1's hook, giving the 10-section arc a closed loop rather than an open-ended list.
